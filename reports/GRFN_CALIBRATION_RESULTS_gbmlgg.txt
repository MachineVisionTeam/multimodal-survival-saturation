================================================================================
GRFN-Pathomic on TCGA-GBMLGG — Phase 2c CALIBRATION AUDIT
Paper-style report — 2026-05-18
================================================================================

STATUS

Phase 2c (15-fold paired calibration audit of GRFN-Pathomic vs locked
Pathomic Fusion baseline on TCGA-GBMLGG) is COMPLETE. The pre-registered
hypothesis (IBS gain at flat c-Index) was CLEANLY FALSIFIED.

This document captures (i) the experimental setup, (ii) the per-fold
calibration metrics, (iii) the paired statistical analysis, (iv) the
mechanistic diagnosis of why GRFN under Cox failed at calibration, and
(v) the resulting reframe of the saturation paper from one architectural
lever to three independent levers — architecture / encoder / loss.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TL;DR  (one paragraph)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

GRFN-Pathomic, an evidential Gaussian-Random-Fuzzy-Number fusion module
trained with Cox partial likelihood, scored WORSE on Integrated Brier
Score than the locked Pathomic Fusion baseline (GRFN IBS 0.1049 +/- 0.0130
vs PF 0.0971 +/- 0.0103; mean Delta = +0.0078, paired-t p = 0.005,
Wilcoxon p = 0.0006; GRFN beat PF on IBS in 1 of 15 folds). It also scored
worse than post-hoc-Platt-scaled PF (mean Delta = +0.0103, paired-t p =
0.0003). The result is a clean falsification of the pre-registered
prediction (P(IBS gain) ~= 0.65) and is mechanistically explained: the
GRFN combination rule produces calibrated channels (sigma_f^2, h_f) but
these are gradient-free under Cox, which trains only mu_f. The Breslow
estimator used to produce S(t|x) for IBS computation also consumes only
mu_f, so the calibration superstructure is mathematically guaranteed to
be discarded. This finding does NOT diminish the saturation paper — it
strengthens it, by adding a second axis of evidence: c-Index ceiling
(8 fusion families) AND calibration ceiling (architectural calibration
fixes do not work under Cox).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The pre-registered hypothesis
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

From GRFN_Pathomic_ARCHITECTURE.txt section 7 (and confirmed by the
external Theoretically-Distinct Fusion Mechanisms survey, May 2026):

   "P(IBS gain at flat c-Index | flat Delta c-Index) ~= 0.65"

   Reasoning:
   - The Cox + concordance loss is known to dis-incentivize calibrated
     probability output (Ghawami audit, arXiv 2604.04239, April 2026:
     'MCAT achieves c-Index 0.817 on GBMLGG yet fails 1-calibration on
     all 5 folds').
   - The GRFN combination rule yields calibrated uncertainty by
     construction; the per-modality reliability r_m and epistemic
     precision h_m are mathematically defined Dempster-Shafer evidence
     combinations.
   - Therefore, given equal c-Index, GRFN-Pathomic should produce
     materially better IBS than PF.

This was tested as Phase 2c, after Phase 2 (15-fold c-Index paired
comparison) showed GRFN and PF were statistically indistinguishable on
discrimination (GRFN 0.8134 +/- 0.0695 vs PF 0.8174 +/- 0.0717, Delta
= -0.0040, p = 0.43). The c-Index parity was the prerequisite condition
for the calibration test to be informative.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Experimental setup
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Cohort                : TCGA-GBMLGG, 770 patients, 15-fold cross-validation
                        (PF-published splits, identical to Phase 2)
Trained models        :
  PF baseline          : 15 fold checkpoints from locked replication
                         (runs_modern/.../pathgraphomic_fusion/),
                         c-Index 0.8174 +/- 0.0717
  GRFN-Pathomic        : 15 fold checkpoints from Phase 2 sweep
                         (runs_modern/.../pathgraphomic_grfn/),
                         c-Index 0.8134 +/- 0.0695
  PF + Platt scaling   : Post-hoc Platt logistic recalibration applied
                         per evaluation time on the training set; baseline
                         recommended by the Ghawami audit paper
Calibration metrics   :
  Integrated Brier Score (IBS) : sksurv.metrics.integrated_brier_score
                                 over 50-point grid spanning the 10th-80th
                                 percentile of event times.
  Integrated Binomial Log-     : IPCW-weighted approximation of binomial
    Likelihood (IBLL)            log-likelihood per evaluation time,
                                 trapezoidal-integrated over the same grid.
Survival-curve extraction:
  Both PF and GRFN produce a scalar Cox risk. To compute S(t|x) for IBS,
  the Breslow non-parametric baseline survival is fit on the TRAINING
  fold's risks + survival, then applied to TEST-fold risks. This is the
  standard Cox-PH calibration workflow. CRITICALLY, the Breslow estimator
  consumes ONLY the scalar risk; any auxiliary GRFN outputs (sigma_f^2,
  h_f) are not seen.
Patient-level aggregation:
  Each PF/GRFN training run produces patch-level predictions (~15 patches
  per patient). To compute patient-level metrics, predictions are averaged
  within each patient (group by x_patname from the saved data dict). This
  matches Phase 2's c-Index aggregation.
Code                  : GRFN-Pathomic/gbmlgg/calibration_audit.py
Run-time              : ~30 seconds for all 15 folds + Platt baselines.

4. Mechanistic diagnosis — why GRFN under Cox fails calibration
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The GRFN module exposes three channels per prediction:
   mu_f      - fused Cox-comparable risk (scalar)
   sigma_f^2 - aleatoric variance (calibration channel 1)
   h_f       - fused epistemic precision (calibration channel 2)

Under Cox partial likelihood, the loss is computed as
   L_cox = - sum_{i: event=1} [ risk_i - log sum_j exp(risk_j) 1{t_j >= t_i} ]
where risk_i is a scalar — specifically mu_f for GRFN.

This means:
  - The Cox loss gradient flows back through mu_f only.
  - The sigma_f^2 and h_f channels are computed by the GRFN combination
    rule but NEVER appear in the loss expression.
  - Backpropagation cannot adjust the parameters that produce sigma_f^2
    or h_f from any signal other than the (indirect) regularisation
    effect of the shared encoder layers.

For IBS computation, S(t|x) is constructed via the Breslow estimator:
   S(t|x) = S_0(t)^{exp(mu_f(x))}
where S_0(t) is the baseline survival function fit non-parametrically
on training-set (mu_f, time, event) triples.

This means:
  - Only mu_f is used to construct S(t|x). sigma_f^2 and h_f are not
    inputs to the Breslow estimator.
  - The "calibrated uncertainty" GRFN provides is structurally invisible
    to the calibration metric.

In short: GRFN-under-Cox is mathematically equivalent to a single-scalar
Cox model with extra unused channels. The pre-registered hypothesis
implicitly assumed that the GRFN combination rule's calibration would
propagate to the metric. It does not. Both training (Cox loss) and
evaluation (Breslow + IBS) consume only mu_f, so calibration channels
have no path of influence on observed performance.

A secondary contributor: GRFN applies a sigmoid + linear-scale
activation (sigmoid(z) * 6 - 3) on mu_f to match PF's hazard convention,
which further compresses the risk distribution relative to PF's
unbounded Linear classifier output. A narrower risk distribution
produces a coarser Breslow baseline and therefore worse Brier score.
But this is a second-order effect; the primary issue is the loss
function.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Interpretation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1  This is a clean falsification, not a noisy null.

  - The Delta IBS is significantly DIFFERENT from zero (p = 0.005),
    not statistically indistinguishable.
  - The direction is consistent across folds (14/15 show GRFN worse).
  - The mechanism is identifiable a priori: the calibration channels
    are gradient-free under Cox.
  - The result is reproducible: re-running the calibration audit gives
    identical numbers (per-fold pkl files saved at
    GRFN-Pathomic/gbmlgg/CALIBRATION_RESULTS.pkl).

This is the strongest possible falsification outcome — it is not "noise
swamped any signal", it is "the signal goes in the wrong direction with
high confidence and we know why".

5.2  This STRENGTHENS the saturation paper, not weakens it.

The previous saturation argument was:
   "8 fusion architectures (GenoFiLM x 3, FiLM_residual, NoGate
    Trilinear, GBT-Fusion, UNI2-h encoder, DAF, HACA) fail to break the
    c-Index ceiling on TCGA-GBMLGG. The ceiling is data-bound (~4%
    interaction variance, InterSHAP)."

After Phase 2c, the argument becomes:
   "9 architectures fail to break the c-Index ceiling, AND a 10th
    architecture specifically designed for calibrated uncertainty fails
    to break the CALIBRATION ceiling under Cox partial likelihood.
    Architectural fusion changes cannot rescue calibration either.
    Two independent levers (architecture, encoder) are demonstrably
    closed. The remaining lever (loss function) is tested separately
    via DSM-Pathomic."

The reframe converts the paper from a single-lever survey to a
three-lever map, which is a substantively stronger artifact.

5.3  This matches the audit paper's recommended fix.

  Ghawami's audit (arXiv 2604.04239) reported that no fusion
  architecture in their 11-model benchmark achieves both good c-Index
  and good calibration. Their recommended interim solution was
  post-hoc Platt scaling. Phase 2c shows directly WHY architectural
  fixes don't work: the calibration channels of even an explicitly
  evidential architecture are gradient-blind under Cox. Platt is
  effective precisely because it sidesteps the loss function entirely.
  Our result therefore validates the audit's recommendation with
  causal evidence.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. What this means for the project
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Implications:

  i.   GRFN-Pathomic occupies the saturation paper's "architectural
       calibration fix under Cox" row. It is NOT a positive
       contribution. It is the 9th row of the saturation evidence
       ladder.

  ii.  The Cox-PH reformulation of GRFN, originally framed as the
       paper's load-bearing novelty claim, is now correctly framed as
       a falsification mechanism: "we re-derived EsurvFusion's
       evidential combination rule to be Cox-compatible specifically
       to test whether the calibration claim survives under Cox.
       It did not."

  iii. DSM-Pathomic (originally specified as the secondary arm) is
       PROMOTED to the load-bearing arm. It is the only experiment
       that can isolate the loss-function lever.

  iv.  GRFN with its native log-time Gaussian likelihood (the
       companion arm specified in DSM_Pathomic_ARCHITECTURE.txt section
       6) is the mechanistic confirmation experiment: if GRFN with
       a likelihood-based loss DOES improve calibration, it proves
       that the Phase 2c failure was Cox-specific, not architecture-
       specific.

  v.   The TOMM submission's headline shifts from "GRFN provides the
       missing calibration fix" to "we map three independent levers
       of fusion saturation on TCGA-GBMLGG and demonstrate that
       architecture is closed, calibration channels are closed under
       Cox, and only the loss function remains untested by us."
