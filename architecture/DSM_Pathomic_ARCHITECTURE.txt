================================================================================
DSM-Pathomic — Modality-Specific Deep Survival Machines on Pathomic Fusion
Architecture design document — 2026-05-18
================================================================================

Status: design only. No code yet. Pre-implementation review.

Decision context: GRFN-Pathomic Phase 2c (calibration audit, 2026-05-18)
falsified the pre-registered hypothesis that an evidential combination rule
would deliver calibration gains under Cox partial likelihood. Result:
GRFN IBS 0.1049 +/- 0.0130 vs PF 0.0971 +/- 0.0103 (Δ = +0.0078, paired-t
p = 0.005; loss in 14/15 folds). Mechanistic diagnosis: GRFN's calibration
channels (σ²_f, h_f) are computed by the combination rule but never enter
the Cox loss gradient, so the architectural calibration guarantee is
discarded. This pivots the saturation paper from a single architectural
lever to THREE independent levers — architecture / encoder / loss — and
makes DSM-Pathomic (the loss-function lever) the load-bearing remaining
experiment.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. TL;DR (one paragraph)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DSM-Pathomic replaces both PF's Cox partial likelihood AND its
TrilinearFusion_A block with a 3K-component Weibull mixture (Deep Survival
Machines; Nagpal, Li, Dubrawski, IEEE JBHI 25(8):3163-3175, 2021) in which
each component's shape/scale parameters are predicted by a SINGLE modality
and the mixture weights are predicted by the concatenated embedding. The
training objective is the DSM mixture log-likelihood — a parametric survival
likelihood that does *not* throw away calibration information the way Cox
does. The model produces full S(t|x) at any time t, so IBS/IBLL are
computable natively without a Breslow estimator. ~1,920 parameters at the
fusion+head stage vs PF's ~3.57M (~1,860x reduction), with the locked
PF encoders (PathNet/VGG, GraphNet/GCN, MaxNet/SNN) unchanged and frozen.
This is the saturation paper's loss-function row: the only known way to
test whether the calibration ceiling is Cox-specific or deeper.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Why DSM-Pathomic exists (and why it exists NOW)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1  The pre-registered falsification

The GRFN-Pathomic design doc (HACA_DESIGN_v1_5 / GRFN_Pathomic_ARCHITECTURE)
predicted:
   "P(IBS gain at flat c-Index | flat Δ c-Index) ≈ 0.65"
   "GRFN's combination rule produces calibrated uncertainty by construction.
    Cox + concordance optimization actively dis-incentivizes calibration.
    So you're essentially guaranteed to improve IBS over MCAT unless the
    Cox-mu_f loss flattens μ_f to a single proportional-hazards regression."

The Phase 2c experimental result: 15-fold paired IBS audit on GBMLGG.

  Method                          IBS              Δ vs PF
  -----------------------------------------------------------
  PF (locked baseline)            0.0971 ± 0.0103
  PF + post-hoc Platt scaling     0.0946 ± 0.0099  -0.0025
  GRFN-Pathomic                   0.1049 ± 0.0130  +0.0078
                                                   (paired-t p = 0.005,
                                                    Wilcoxon p = 0.0006)
  GRFN won (lower IBS) in 1/15 folds.

The "Cox-mu_f loss flattens μ_f" branch of the design prediction was the
correct one: σ²_f and h_f, the GRFN calibration channels, are computed by
the GRFN combination rule but never enter the Cox loss gradient. The
Breslow estimator that produces calibrated S(t|x) for IBS computation
consumes only μ_f. The sigmoid+[-3,+3] squashing further compresses μ_f's
distribution relative to PF's unbounded linear classifier. The result is
mathematically inevitable: GRFN under Cox is equivalent to a single-scalar
Cox predictor with extra unused channels.

This is a CLEAN MECHANISTIC FALSIFICATION, not a noisy null. The architectural
calibration channel is real (the σ²_f, h_f are computed and bounded), but it
is gradient-free under Cox.

1.2  What that result implies for the saturation paper

Before Phase 2c, the saturation paper had one lever (architecture):
   "8 fusion families tested. All fail. The c-Index ceiling is data-bound."

After Phase 2c, the paper has THREE independent levers:
   "Three levers — architecture, encoder, and loss function — and at least
    two of them (architecture, encoder) are closed against meaningfully
    breaking either the c-Index or the calibration ceiling on TCGA-GBMLGG.
    The third lever (loss function) is open and is tested here."

DSM-Pathomic is the loss-function row. It is the only remaining lever
that has not been systematically tested on the locked PF baseline.

1.3  Why DSM specifically (not DeepHit, not DeepSurv, not full-likelihood-GRFN)

Three reasons DSM is the right choice for THIS test:

  (a) DSM produces a parametric S(t|x) at any time, so IBS/IBLL are
      computable natively from the predicted mixture without Breslow.
      The Cox+Breslow workaround that compressed GRFN's calibration is
      eliminated by construction.

  (b) DSM's mixture-of-Weibulls form has a CALIBRATED LIKELIHOOD by
      design — the negative log-likelihood directly penalises miscalibrated
      S(t|x). This is the converse of Cox's concordance-optimising
      partial likelihood that the Ghawami audit (arXiv 2604.04239)
      identified as the source of the calibration failure.

  (c) The mixture form gives a clean modality-attribution channel: each
      Weibull component's parameters are predicted by a single modality,
      so the mixture weights w_{m,k}(x) describe which modality's
      survival distribution drives each patient's prediction. This is
      analogous to GRFN's r_m but at the survival-distribution level,
      not at the risk-scalar level.

The alternative (DeepHit) uses discrete-time hazards with a softmax over
fixed time bins. It is also calibration-aware but introduces
discretisation artefacts. The Ghawami audit explicitly flagged the
discrete 4-bin formulation in MCAT/PORPOISE/SurvPath as a partial source
of GBMLGG miscalibration. DSM's continuous Weibull form avoids this.

The alternative (full-likelihood-GRFN — see Section 6 below) is the
DIAGNOSIS test for the GRFN result, not the saturation test. Both should
run; DSM is the primary saturation row, full-likelihood-GRFN is the
mechanistic confirmation arm.

2. Mathematical specification
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1  Notation
   x  = (x_path, x_graph, x_omic)  - patient input
   h_p, h_g, h_o ∈ R^32           - locked PF modality embeddings
   K                              - number of Weibull components per modality
                                     (default K=4 per design survey
                                      Recommendation 2)
   M = 3                          - number of modalities (path, graph, omic)
   t_i, delta_i                   - survival time and event indicator
                                    (delta_i = 1 if event, 0 if censored)

2.2  Per-modality Weibull mixture component heads (NEW)

   For each modality m ∈ {p, g, o}, a small linear head emits 2K positive
   parameters (K shape, K scale):
       [logα_{m,1}, ..., logα_{m,K}, logβ_{m,1}, ..., logβ_{m,K}]
           = Linear_m(h_m)            Linear(32 → 2K)
       α_{m,k} = exp(logα_{m,k}).clamp(0.5, 5.0)    Weibull shape
       β_{m,k} = exp(logβ_{m,k}).clamp(0.1, 100.0)  Weibull scale

   Shape clamps prevent the Weibull from degenerating (α < 0.5 gives
   pathological decreasing-hazard, α > 5 gives near-deterministic time);
   scale clamps prevent numerical overflow on log(t). Standard ranges
   from auton-survival's reference DSM implementation.

   Each head: 32 × 2K weight + 2K bias = (32 × 2K + 2K) params
   For K=4: 264 params × 3 modalities = 792 params.

2.3  Mixture weight head (NEW)

   The 3K mixture weights are predicted from the CONCATENATED embedding,
   so the gating sees all three modalities (analogous to GRFN's contextual
   reliability head):
       logits = Linear_w(cat(h_p, h_g, h_o))   Linear(96 → 3K)
       w(x) = softmax(logits)                  shape (3K,), sums to 1

   For K=4 (3K=12): 96 × 12 + 12 = 1,164 params.

2.4  DSM survival function

   The fused survival function at time t > 0:

       S(t | x) = Σ_{m ∈ {p,g,o}} Σ_{k=1..K}
                    w_{m,k}(x) · S_Weibull(t; α_{m,k}(x_m), β_{m,k}(x_m))

   where the single-component Weibull survival function is:
       S_Weibull(t; α, β) = exp(-(t / β)^α)

   The mixture is over 3K = 12 components (for K=4). Each component is
   parameterised by a single modality; the weights are contextual (depend
   on all three).

   Important: w_{m,k}(x) is the mixture weight for component k of modality m,
   and is predicted from concat(h_p, h_g, h_o). This is the design survey
   Recommendation 2's "modality-specific Deep Survival Machines mixture":
   crucially each component's shape/scale are SINGLE-modality, so the
   mixture component distribution preserves per-modality attribution.

2.5  DSM training loss

   The DSM negative log-likelihood on a batch of N patients:

   L_DSM = -1/N Σ_i [ δ_i log f(t_i | x_i) + (1 - δ_i) log S(t_i | x_i) ]

   where:
       f(t|x) = - d/dt S(t|x)
              = Σ_{m,k} w_{m,k}(x) · (α/β) (t/β)^(α-1) exp(-(t/β)^α)
                                                 (per-component Weibull density)

   For numerical stability use log-domain computations (log_w, log_S,
   logsumexp). Reference: auton-survival's `dsm.losses.conditional_loss`.

   Optional regularisation: ELBO-style term penalising deviation of
   mixture weights from a learned prior (auton-survival's "discount"
   hyperparameter). Default: disabled — adds tuning surface without
   clear benefit for our paired-comparison protocol.

2.6  Cox-comparable risk for c-Index reporting

   For backward compatibility with the saturation paper's c-Index column,
   we report a scalar risk derived from S(t|x). Standard choice:

       risk_i = -E[T_i]
              = -Σ_{m,k} w_{m,k}(x_i) · β_{m,k} · Γ(1 + 1/α_{m,k})

   where E[T] for a Weibull(α, β) is β·Γ(1 + 1/α). Negative because
   "higher risk → shorter expected time."

   Concordance is then computed on these risks against (t_i, δ_i) using
   sksurv.metrics.concordance_index_censored, identical to PF/GRFN.

2.7  Predicted hazard for ablations (not used in loss)

   For Phase 4 ablation studies (modality attribution), we can also report
   the per-modality marginal survival:
       S_m(t | x) = Σ_{k=1..K} w_{m,k}(x) · S_Weibull(t; α_{m,k}, β_{m,k}) / Σ_k w_{m,k}(x)
   re-normalised within each modality's component set. This gives a
   per-modality predicted survival curve — direct attribution for the
   "which modality's distribution did this patient's prediction come from"
   question.

3. Where it sits in the locked Pathomic Fusion pipeline
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PF pipeline (TrilinearFusion_A + Cox):

   x_path  → PathNet/VGG  → h_p ∈ R^32                LOCKED
   x_graph → GraphNet     → h_g ∈ R^32                LOCKED
   x_omic  → MaxNet/SNN   → h_o ∈ R^32                LOCKED

   ↓ bilinear gating + Kronecker product + encoder1+2  REPLACED
   ↓ Linear classifier                                  REPLACED
   ↓ sigmoid * 6 - 3 activation                         REMOVED
   ↓ Cox partial-likelihood loss                        REPLACED

DSM-Pathomic pipeline:

   x_path  → PathNet/VGG  → h_p ∈ R^32                LOCKED (same as PF)
   x_graph → GraphNet     → h_g ∈ R^32                LOCKED (same as PF)
   x_omic  → MaxNet/SNN   → h_o ∈ R^32                LOCKED (same as PF)

   ↓ Linear_p(h_p) → (α_{p,1..K}, β_{p,1..K})       NEW (264 params)
   ↓ Linear_g(h_g) → (α_{g,1..K}, β_{g,1..K})       NEW (264 params)
   ↓ Linear_o(h_o) → (α_{o,1..K}, β_{o,1..K})       NEW (264 params)
   ↓ Linear_w(cat(h_p,h_g,h_o)) → 3K mixture weights NEW (1,164 params)
   ↓ DSM Weibull-mixture S(t|x), f(t|x)              closed form
   ↓ DSM negative log-likelihood                     REPLACES Cox loss

   For c-Index reporting:
   risk_i = -E_w[β · Γ(1 + 1/α)]                     scalar, for concordance

   For IBS reporting:
   S(t|x) evaluated at eval_times                    direct, no Breslow

Encoders stay frozen and identical to PF. Splits, modality data
preprocessing, and the train/test convention are unchanged. The only
differences from PF are the head + the loss.

4. Parameter count comparison
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PF (TrilinearFusion_A + encoder1 + encoder2 + classifier):
   bilinear_z heads + skip + Kronecker encoder + classifier
                                                  ≈ 3,573,601 params

GRFN-Pathomic (3 modality heads + reliability head, parameter-free combo):
                                                  ≈ 588 params

DSM-Pathomic (3 modality Weibull heads + mixture gate, K=4):
   3 × (32 × 8 + 8) + (96 × 12 + 12)              ≈ 1,956 params

   (vs design survey's quoted ~1,920 — the small discrepancy is whether
    you count biases; same order of magnitude either way.)

DSM-Pathomic fusion+head block is ~1,827× smaller than PF's fusion+head
block and ~3× larger than GRFN's. Same order-of-magnitude story: extreme
parameter reduction with no clear architectural advantage to PF.

5. Required ablations
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A1.  K sweep: K ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}.
     - K=1 reduces to a per-modality Weibull AFT model (no mixture).
     - K=4 is the default (auton-survival's reference choice).
     - K=8 tests whether more components rescue calibration.
     If K=1 already gives the IBS gain, the mixture is unnecessary and
     the contribution is "Weibull likelihood beats Cox" not "modality-
     specific mixture beats Cox" — that distinction matters for the paper.

A2.  Cross-modality mixture (each component's α/β predicted from concat of all
     three modalities instead of one).
     This is the design survey's "critical ablation" — recovers exactly
     the MMDCSM (Wen et al., ICCVW 2025) configuration and isolates the
     modality-attribution claim. If A2 gives same IBS as the default,
     the attribution part of DSM-Pathomic is decorative.

A3.  Replace Weibull with Log-Normal parametric form.
     Sanity check on parametric form. Same DSM structure, different
     distribution family. Tells us whether the gain is from "any
     parametric likelihood" or "Weibull specifically."

A4.  Modality lesion runs (drop each of path/graph/omic by zeroing).
     Check that DSM's mixture weights w_{m,*} concentrate on the
     remaining modalities, matching GRFN's reliability-saturation
     observation.

A5.  Calibration metric battery: IBS, IBLL, D-calibration (Haider et al.
     2020), Hosmer-Lemeshow at the GBMLGG event-time quintiles
     (matches the Ghawami audit protocol). Required for direct
     comparability with the audit paper's claim of "MCAT fails 1-calibration
     on all 5 GBMLGG folds."

A6.  c-Index reporting at the standard PF / GRFN convention (negative
     expected survival time as risk).
     For paired comparison with PF and GRFN.

A7.  Cox-comparable substitution: train DSM with K=1 AND replace the
     DSM negative log-likelihood with Cox partial-likelihood on -E[T].
     This is the critical isolation experiment — if it matches PF's IBS,
     then the entire DSM gain is from the likelihood, not the mixture
     structure. Conversely, if it matches PF's c-Index, then the
     architectural mixture doesn't change discrimination.

6. Companion experiment — full-likelihood GRFN (mechanistic confirmation arm)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DSM tests the LOSS-FUNCTION lever generically. To confirm that the GRFN
calibration channel WOULD have helped under the right loss, run an
additional companion experiment:

   GRFN-Pathomic with Gaussian survival likelihood on log(t):
     L_GRFN_native = -log N( log(t_i) | mu_f(x_i), sigma_f²(x_i) ) * delta_i
                    + censored_term

   This is EsurvFusion's actual training objective (arXiv 2412.01215v1
   Section 4), which uses a Gaussian likelihood on log-time rather than
   Cox. It propagates gradient through ALL THREE GRFN channels (μ_f, σ_f²,
   h_f) and through the per-modality reliabilities r_m.

   Implementation cost: ~50 LOC on top of existing GRFN code. ~2 days.
   Status in the paper: secondary mechanistic-confirmation row, NOT the
   primary loss-function lever (DSM is).

The companion experiment lets us distinguish two narratives:

  Narrative A (DSM wins on IBS, GRFN-native-likelihood also wins):
     "The Cox loss specifically discards calibration. Any likelihood-based
     loss recovers it. The architectural details (DSM vs GRFN-native) are
     interchangeable; the loss is the lever."

  Narrative B (DSM wins on IBS, GRFN-native-likelihood does not):
     "The mixture-of-Weibulls form is the right inductive bias for
     calibrated survival. Gaussian-on-log-time, despite being a
     likelihood, is not. The Weibull mixture is doing real work."

Both are publishable. Narrative A is the cleaner saturation-paper finding
(the loss is the lever); Narrative B is a richer scientific story.

If DSM AND GRFN-native both null on IBS, we have the strongest possible
saturation finding: even the loss-function lever is closed. That is the
TOMM submission's strongest pitch.

8. Honest probability assessment
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pre-experiment priors (subject to update once Phase 2 c-Index comes in):

  Outcome                                                  P
  ---------------------------------------------------------------
  c-Index delta vs PF on GBMLGG >= +0.012                  0.08
  c-Index delta in [-0.005, +0.005] (statistical parity)   0.55
  IBS delta vs PF on GBMLGG (DSM lower) >= 0.005           0.45
  IBS delta on GBMLGG roughly equal to PF (Cox is fine?)   0.30
  IBS delta on GBMLGG worse than PF (likelihood not the lever) 0.25
  c-Index parity AND IBS gain (the "Narrative A" outcome)  0.30
  c-Index parity AND IBS null (the saturation strongest)   0.25
  c-Index parity AND IBS worse (mixture overfits)          0.20

  P(publishable saturation-paper row): 1.00. All three outcomes (IBS
  gain / IBS null / IBS worse) inform the three-lever framing.

Failure modes to monitor in Phase 0/1:
  - Weibull α collapses to the clamp boundaries (α → 0.5 or α → 5)
    across all components. Diagnostic: histogram of α values per epoch.
    Mitigation: lower the upper clamp, or use a Beta prior on α.
  - Mixture weights w_{m,k}(x) collapse to one component (the GRFN-style
    collapse failure mode). Diagnostic: per-epoch entropy of the
    softmax(logits). If entropy → 0, add entropy regularisation.
  - β scale explodes. Already clamped to 100; monitor for hits on the
    upper boundary.
  - DSM loss returns NaN. Most common cause: t_i = 0 in the data
    (some patients censored at admission). Add small epsilon to t_i in
    the log calculations.

11. What DSM-Pathomic is NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - **Not a new architecture.** The DSM model is from Nagpal et al. 2021.
    See §0.5 for the explicit novelty inventory.
  - Not a c-Index headline play (P(meaningful gain) = 0.08).
  - Not a replacement for the architectural saturation row. DSM tests the
    LOSS lever; GRFN-Pathomic remains the saturation paper's architectural
    row under Cox.
  - Not an encoder upgrade (encoders remain locked).
  - Not a fix for the GRFN calibration result. To prove the GRFN
    architecture's calibration channels can work given a correct loss,
    see the companion experiment (Section 6).
  - Not MMDCSM as published. MMDCSM (Wen et al., ICCVW 2025) uses
    cross-modality components (each component conditioned on all three
    modalities). DSM-Pathomic's modality-specific components are an
    incremental structural change for per-modality attribution; ablation
    A2 (§5) recovers MMDCSM exactly.
  - Not standalone-paper material. The novelty profile is too thin
    (incremental config over MMDCSM). Its value is as the loss-function
    row of the saturation paper, where the publishable contribution is
    the experimental design, not this individual architecture.
