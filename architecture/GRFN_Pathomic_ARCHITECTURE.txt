================================================================================
GRFN-Pathomic — Gaussian Random Fuzzy Number Fusion for Pathomic Survival
Architecture design document — 2026-05-17
================================================================================

Status: design only. No code yet. Pre-implementation review.

Decision context: arrived at GRFN-Pathomic after (a) HACA-on-MCAT pilot on
BLCA produced a clean saturation falsification (r_j spread + c-Index drop
under Option-C), and (b) a Theoretically-Distinct Fusion Mechanisms survey
ranked GRFN-Pathomic as the highest expected-information arm among
remaining-to-try fusion families, given the user's TOMM Aug 31 deadline and
the "Responsible and Explainable Multimodal Fusion" special-issue call.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. TL;DR  (one paragraph)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GRFN-Pathomic replaces Pathomic Fusion's gated bilinear + Kronecker fusion
block with a Gaussian-Random-Fuzzy-Number (GRFN) evidential combination rule
in decision space. Each of the three modality embeddings (path, graph, omic)
emits a fuzzy survival prediction (mean mu_i, aleatoric variance sigma^2_i,
epistemic precision h_i) plus a learned reliability scalar r_i in [0,1]; the
three predictions are combined via a closed-form weighted-evidence rule into
a single (mu_f, sigma_f^2, h_f). The Cox partial likelihood is computed from
mu_f. The model is roughly 60-fold smaller than PF's Kronecker block at the
fusion stage (~600 new params vs ~36K-3.5M for PF's bilinear+Kronecker+
encoders), produces native per-modality reliability attributions and
per-prediction epistemic confidence as side outputs, and is theoretically
distinct from every fusion mechanism in the user's prior 7-intervention list
(gating, FiLM, Kronecker, attention, encoder-swap, disagreement-reconcili-
ation, hazard-anchored attention). The publishable contribution is robust to
c-Index outcome: even at flat c-Index, the calibration story (IBS, IBLL) and
the r_i attribution story carry the TOMM submission on the explainability
axis.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lineage of the idea
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Direct ancestor:
  Huang, Quost, Denoeux et al.
  "EsurvFusion: Evidential Survival Analysis via Gaussian Random Fuzzy
  Numbers." IEEE TFS 34(1):76-88, 2026.
  DOI: 10.1109/TFUZZ.2025.3623879   arXiv: 2412.01215v1
  Code: not publicly released as of 2026-05-17.

EsurvFusion is the published reference for GRFN-based survival fusion. Their
implementation: 3 tabular inputs (clinical, omics, sometimes images-as-
tabular), regression-style log-time targets, Brier score evaluation on
HECKTOR2022, TCGA-BRCA, TCGA-BLCA, TCGA-COADREAD. Crucially, EsurvFusion
does NOT evaluate on TCGA-GBMLGG or TCGA-KIRC — the exact cohorts where
Pathomic Fusion has the locked baseline.

What GRFN-Pathomic does NOT inherit from EsurvFusion:
  - tabular preprocessing (we keep PF's locked 32-d learned modality embeddings)
  - regression-style endpoint (we use Cox partial likelihood, matching PF)
  - Brier-score-only evaluation (we report c-Index + IBS + IBLL together)
  - GBMLGG and KIRC are open ground (no GRFN baseline exists)

What GRFN-Pathomic adds beyond EsurvFusion:
  - First Cox-partial-likelihood reformulation of the GRFN combination rule
  - First TCGA-GBMLGG and TCGA-KIRC GRFN-style numbers
  - Built on PF's frozen 32-d encoders → fusion-only contribution is isolated
  - r_i sweep + h_i ablation as primary explainability experiments


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Mathematical specification
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1  Notation
   x  = (x_path, x_graph, x_omic)  — patient input
   h_p, h_g, h_o ∈ R^32  — locked PF modality embeddings (NOT retrained)
   K = 3   — number of modalities
   N      — training-batch size
   t_i, delta_i  — survival time and event indicator for patient i

2.2  Per-modality fuzzy survival prediction

For each modality m ∈ {p, g, o}, a small MLP-free linear head emits three
scalars:

   [mu_m, lsv_m, lh_m] = Linear_m(h_m)            shape (3,)
   sigma_m^2 = exp(lsv_m)                          aleatoric variance > 0
   h_m       = exp(lh_m)                           epistemic precision > 0
   mu_m                                            survival-risk mean (real)

The per-modality fuzzy prediction is the Gaussian Random Fuzzy Number
(Denoeux, IFSA 2009; Huang et al., IEEE TFS 2026):

   Y_m ~ GRFN(mu_m, sigma_m^2, h_m)

with membership function (Eq. 3 of arXiv 2412.01215v1):

   Y^_m(t) = exp[ -(h_m / 2) * (t - mu_m)^2 ]

The scalar mu_m plays the role of a "modality-specific Cox risk."

2.3  Learned modality reliability

A separate Linear head, taking the concatenation of all three modality
embeddings, emits three logits:

   [s_p, s_g, s_o] = Linear_r(concat(h_p, h_g, h_o))      shape (3,)
   r_m = sigmoid(s_m)                                       reliability ∈ (0, 1)

r_m is a learned, *per-patient* scalar that discounts modality m's
contribution. This is the Dempster-Shafer-style discounting (Shafer 1976;
Denoeux 2008). It is the model's first-class explainability output —
analogous to attention weights in MCAT, but with a probabilistic semantics
(reliability is calibrated against the GRFN combination rule, not learned
purely to maximise loss).

2.4  GRFN combination rule  (Eq. 4 of arXiv 2412.01215v1)

Let r_m h_m  be the *discounted precision* of modality m:

   h_f       = Σ_m  r_m h_m                                 epistemic confidence in fused prediction
   mu_f      = ( Σ_m  r_m h_m mu_m )  /  ( Σ_m  r_m h_m )    fused risk
   sigma_f^2 = ( Σ_m  r_m^2  h_m^2  sigma_m^2 )  /  ( Σ_m  r_m h_m )^2
                                                            propagated aleatoric variance

This is a closed-form, differentiable, parameter-free combination rule —
not a learned fusion block. All inductive bias is in the rule.

Special cases:
   r_m ≡ 1  →   precision-weighted mean of mu_m (Bayesian mixture)
   h_m ≡ 1  →   reliability-weighted mean of mu_m (Dempster-Shafer mixture)
   r_m ≡ 1, h_m ≡ 1  →  uniform mean of mu_m (trivial late fusion)

The three special cases are required ablations (section 5 below).

2.5  Cox partial likelihood

Train mu_f as the Cox risk:

   L_Cox  =  - Σ_{i : delta_i = 1}  [ mu_f(x_i)  -  log Σ_{j : t_j ≥ t_i}  exp(mu_f(x_j)) ]

No mixture, no NLL, no Weibull — exactly the same Cox loss PF already uses
in train_test.py:61 (CoxLoss(survtime, censor, pred, device)).

2.6  Side outputs (for downstream explainability + calibration)

For each patient at inference time the model emits:
   - mu_f       (Cox risk)
   - sigma_f^2  (aleatoric variance — disagreement of modality means)
   - h_f        (epistemic confidence — sum of discounted precisions)
   - r_p, r_g, r_o  (per-patient per-modality reliability ∈ [0,1])

These are reported alongside c-Index in all tables.

3. Where it sits in the locked Pathomic Fusion pipeline
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PF pipeline (from /home/sbarua/Region_based_segmentation/pathomic_fusion_replica/
PathomicFusion/fusion.py:57-123 — TrilinearFusion_A):

   x_path   → VGG  → h_p ∈ R^32                                LOCKED
   x_graph  → GCN  → h_g ∈ R^32                                LOCKED
   x_omic   → SNN  → h_o ∈ R^32                                LOCKED

   ↓  bilinear gating (3 × nn.Bilinear)                        REPLACED
   ↓  three gated outputs o1, o2, o3 ∈ R^33                    REPLACED
   ↓  Kronecker product (o1 ⊗ o2 ⊗ o3) ∈ R^{33×33×33}          REPLACED
   ↓  encoder1: Linear(35937 → 96) + ReLU + Dropout            REPLACED
   ↓  skip-concat with (o1, o2, o3)                            REPLACED
   ↓  encoder2: Linear(96+skip → 96) + ReLU + Dropout          REPLACED
   ↓  classifier: Linear(96 → 1)                               REPLACED

   ↓  Cox loss on the scalar risk                              KEPT

GRFN-Pathomic pipeline:

   x_path   → VGG  → h_p ∈ R^32                                LOCKED (same as PF)
   x_graph  → GCN  → h_g ∈ R^32                                LOCKED (same as PF)
   x_omic   → SNN  → h_o ∈ R^32                                LOCKED (same as PF)

   ↓  Linear_p(h_p) → (mu_p, lsv_p, lh_p)   ∈ R^3              NEW (32×3 + 3 = 99 params)
   ↓  Linear_g(h_g) → (mu_g, lsv_g, lh_g)   ∈ R^3              NEW (99 params)
   ↓  Linear_o(h_o) → (mu_o, lsv_o, lh_o)   ∈ R^3              NEW (99 params)
   ↓  Linear_r(cat(h_p,h_g,h_o)) → (s_p,s_g,s_o) ∈ R^3        NEW (96×3 + 3 = 291 params)
   ↓  r_m = sigmoid(s_m),  h_m = exp(lh_m),  sigma_m^2 = exp(lsv_m)
   ↓  GRFN combination rule (closed-form, parameter-free)      NEW (0 params)
   ↓  scalar risk = mu_f

   ↓  Cox loss on mu_f                                         KEPT (PF's existing CoxLoss)

Encoder block stays exactly as in the locked PF replication (VGG 1024×1024-ROI
CNN, graph CNN, SNN over 240 genes). No retraining of the encoders.

4. Parameter count comparison
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pathomic Fusion (TrilinearFusion_A, fusion.py:57-123) — params in the fusion+
classifier block only (encoders excluded):

   linear_h1/h2/h3            : 3 × (32×32 + 32)             ≈ 3,168
   linear_z1/z2/z3 (bilinear) : 3 × (32×32×32 + 32)          ≈ 98,400   ← the big bilinear gates
   linear_o1/o2/o3            : 3 × (32×32 + 32)             ≈ 3,168
   encoder1: Linear(35937 → 96)+bias                          ≈ 3,449,952  ← dominant cost
   encoder2: Linear(195 → 96)+bias                            ≈ 18,816
   classifier: Linear(96 → 1)+bias                            ≈ 97
                                                              ─────────
                                                              ≈ 3,573,601 params

GRFN-Pathomic — fusion+classifier params:

   Linear_p, Linear_g, Linear_o (each 32 → 3, with bias)      : 3 × 99 = 297
   Linear_r (96 → 3, with bias)                                : 291
   GRFN combination rule                                       : 0   (closed form)
   No separate classifier — mu_f IS the risk                  : 0
                                                              ─────────
                                                              ≈ 588 params

GRFN-Pathomic fusion block is ~6,080× smaller than PF's fusion+encoder block.
This is genuine architectural simplification, not merely an extension.

5. Required ablations (each is a saturation-paper row)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A1.  Set r_m ≡ 1 for all m  (no reliability discounting).
     Tests whether learned reliability matters at all. If A1 ≈ default,
     the r_i story is dead and the publishable contribution shifts.

A2.  Set h_m ≡ 1 for all m  (uniform epistemic precision).
     Reduces fusion to a reliability-weighted mean of mu_m. Tests whether
     the GRFN-specific precision channel adds anything beyond DS mixture.

A3.  Single-modality lesion runs:
        - drop h_p (set Linear_p output to zero, freeze)
        - drop h_g
        - drop h_o
     Observe how r_m for the remaining modalities saturates. Confirms that
     r_i is actually a reliability indicator, not a learned bias.

A4.  Permutation-feature-importance audit on r_m.
     For each modality, shuffle its h_m embedding across the batch and
     measure the drop in c-Index. Compare with r_m's average value.
     A monotone relationship between (1 - average r_m) and shuffle-induced
     c-Index drop would be the headline attribution result.

A5.  Calibration audit on the GBMLGG hold-out:
        - Integrated Brier Score (IBS)
        - Integrated Binomial Log-Likelihood (IBLL)
        - 1-calibration plots stratified by predicted risk quintile
     This is the TOMM-aligned headline measurement — IBS gains carry the
     submission even at flat c-Index.

A6.  Sigma_f^2 utility check (post-hoc; uses fixed weights from A5 run):
     Sort patients by sigma_f^2; report whether high-sigma patients are
     systematically harder to predict (longer survival residuals, lower
     individual c-Index contribution). Direct evidence that sigma_f^2
     captures aleatoric uncertainty in a meaningful way.

A7.  h_f utility check (post-hoc):
     Sort patients by h_f; report whether low-h_f (low epistemic
     confidence) patients have higher prediction error. Direct evidence
     of epistemic-confidence calibration.

6. Implementation plan
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Phase 0  — code stub + smoke test (1 day)
  - Create models/grfn_fusion.py with class GRFNFusion(nn.Module).
  - Verify forward returns (mu_f, sigma_f^2, h_f, r) with correct shapes
    on dummy 32-d inputs.
  - Verify gradient flow: each modality head receives gradient, reliability
    head receives gradient, no NaN/Inf.

Phase 1  — wire into the locked PF training loop (1-2 days)
  - In options.py, add fusion choice 'grfn' alongside the existing options.
  - In train_test.py, branch on opt.fusion_type:
        if opt.fusion_type == 'grfn':
            model = build_grfn_model(opt)
        else:
            model = build_pathomic_model(opt)  # existing
  - Cox loss path uses pred = mu_f.
  - Keep all other training hyperparameters identical to the locked PF
    replication (lr, weight decay, epochs, batch size, lambda_cox, etc.).

Phase 2  — paired-seed evaluation on GBMLGG (1 day)
  - 15-fold CV (PF's published split protocol).
  - 3 seeds per fold (matches the 270-paired-run convention used in the
    saturation paper for prior interventions).
  - Output: 45-run c-Index distribution, paired against the locked PF
    baseline at the same fold/seed pairs.
  - Statistical tests: paired t-test, Wilcoxon signed-rank, win-count.

Phase 3  — KIRC cross-cohort confirmation (1 day)
  - Same protocol on KIRC. Locked PF KIRC = 0.7184.
  - Cross-cohort consistency check.

Phase 4  — ablations A1-A7 (3-5 days, mostly unattended)
  - Each ablation reuses the Phase 2/3 infrastructure.
  - Ablation rows go directly into the saturation paper table.

Phase 5  — writeup (2-3 days)
  - Add GRFN-Pathomic row to saturation paper.
  - Calibration story + r_m attribution as the publishable contribution.

Total budget: ~10-12 working days, plus unattended GPU time. Sits cleanly
inside the Aug 31 TOMM deadline.

7. Honest probability assessment (carried over from the design survey)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Outcome                                   Probability
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
c-Index Δ ≥ +0.012 on GBMLGG              ≈ 0.12
c-Index Δ ≥ +0.005 on GBMLGG              ≈ 0.30
Clean informative null (Δ ∈ [−0.005, +0.005]
  with publishable calibration / r_i story) ≈ 0.60
Architectural pathology
  (r_m collapse → all-equal weights, h_m
  collapse → all-equal precision)         ≈ 0.15
80% CI for Δ c-Index on GBMLGG            [−0.010, +0.020]

The expected value of this experiment is in the calibration + attribution
story, NOT in the c-Index gain. P(meaningful calibration improvement at flat
c-Index | Δ c-Index ∈ [−0.005, +0.005]) is high (≈ 0.55) because the GRFN
combination rule produces calibrated uncertainty natively, regardless of
mean accuracy.

The dominant failure mode to watch for during training:
  - r_m collapse: all three r_m → same value (typically near 0.5 or near 1).
    In that case the discounting story is dead. Mitigation: log r_m
    distribution every epoch (same diagnostic pattern as HACA's r_j
    distribution logging in haca_train_utils.py). If r_m collapse happens,
    A1 (set r_m ≡ 1) and the resulting numbers will be statistically
    indistinguishable from the default — that itself is publishable.

  - h_m collapse: all three h_m → same value. Less serious than r_m
    collapse because the combination rule still weights by precision; but
    it removes the per-modality epistemic-confidence signal. A2 (set h_m
    ≡ 1) controls for this.

8. Differentiation from the user's 7 prior interventions
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Prior intervention                       | Family                       | GRFN-Pathomic distinct because…
─────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. GenoFiLM (pre_ln/residual/plain)      | Feature-level multiplicative | GRFN operates in decision space (mu_f), not feature space.
                                         | gating                       | No FiLM-style multiplicative interaction in the fusion block.
2. GBT-Fusion (block-term Tucker)        | Low-rank tensor decomposition| Same axis as PF's Kronecker; GRFN replaces the tensor product
                                         |                              | with a closed-form combination rule, not a low-rank approx.
3. DAF (signed disagreement)             | Decision-level disagreement  | DAF emits a signed disagreement scalar with no calibration
                                         | reconciliation               | semantics. GRFN emits CALIBRATED reliability r_m and
                                         |                              | epistemic precision h_m. Different objects.
4. UNI2-h encoder swap                   | Encoder upgrade              | GRFN-Pathomic keeps the locked VGG/GCN/SNN encoders. The
                                         |                              | contribution is in fusion, not encoders.
5. FiLM_residual replacement             | Feature-level gating variant | Same family as #1.
6. NoGateTrilinear                       | Trilinear bilinear-free      | Same Kronecker space, different gating choice.
7. HACA (hazard-anchored attention)      | Attention with survival bias | HACA modulates a softmax bias; GRFN has no attention at all.
                                         |                              | HACA Option-C (BLCA) gave Δ = −0.0189, falsifying the
                                         |                              | "attention is the bottleneck" hypothesis. GRFN tests an
                                         |                              | orthogonal question: does evidential decision-level fusion
                                         |                              | break the saturation ceiling?

GRFN-Pathomic spans neither feature-level multiplicative interactions, low-
rank tensor decompositions, decision-level disagreement scalars, encoder
upgrades, attention biasing, nor block-term decompositions. It is a new
axis (decision-level evidential combination with intrinsic per-modality
reliability + epistemic precision).

10. What this document is NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Not a paper draft. The saturation paper's GRFN row will be 1-2
    paragraphs + 1 row in the comparison table + a calibration sub-section.
  - Not a final hyperparameter sweep. Phase 1's first run uses learning
    rate, weight decay, batch size, epochs identical to the locked PF
    replication. Hyperparameter tuning of the r-head / h-head is deferred.
  - Not an EsurvFusion clone. We use Cox partial likelihood (PF-style), not
    Brier-score regression (EsurvFusion-style). The Cox-compatible refor-
    mulation of the GRFN combination rule is the single most important
    novelty claim and must be locked down in the writeup explicitly.
  - Not a replacement of the encoders. The 32-d VGG/GCN/SNN encoders that
    locked the PF replication at 0.8174 GBMLGG / 0.7184 KIRC stay frozen.
    Fusion contribution is isolated.

11. Decision-ready next step
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Phase 0 (code stub + smoke test) takes one day. The earliest decision point
is "does the smoke test reproduce the locked PF result when r_m ≡ 1, h_m ≡ 1,
mu_f = mean(mu_p, mu_g, mu_o)?" — i.e. does the model degenerate to
late-mean fusion at the trivial limit. If yes, the implementation is
correct. If no, debug the GRFN combination implementation before any real
training.

After Phase 0 passes, Phase 1 (wire-into-training) is ~1 day. Phase 2 (full
GBMLGG paired-seed run) is ~6 GPU-hours on 4 L40s. The first real number
should arrive within ~2-3 calendar days of greenlight.
